# Strand-seq Protocol


![[Pasted image 20260728123214.png]]

## Overview

**Strand-seq (Single-cell DNA Template Strand Sequencing)** is a single-cell sequencing method that selectively sequences the **parental template DNA strands** while preserving their strand orientation.

Unlike conventional WGS, Strand-seq retains information about whether DNA originates from the **Watson (W)** or **Crick (C)** template strand.

---

## 1. DNA Replication with BrdU

Cells undergo **one round of DNA replication** in the presence of **BrdU (5-bromo-2′-deoxyuridine)**.

During replication:

- The original parental strands remain unlabeled.
- BrdU is incorporated into the **newly synthesized DNA strands**.

Therefore, each sister chromatid contains:

- one original template strand
- one newly synthesized BrdU-containing strand

---

## 2. Mitosis and Sister Chromatid Segregation

After DNA replication, the cells undergo **mitosis**.

The sister chromatids segregate into daughter cells. Because each sister chromatid contains one parental template strand, individual daughter cells inherit different combinations of parental strands.

For a chromosome pair, the possible strand states are:

- **WW** – both homologs represented by Watson template strands
- **CC** – both homologs represented by Crick template strands
- **WC / CW** – one Watson and one Crick template strand

---

## 3. Single-cell Isolation

After mitosis, individual daughter cells are isolated, typically using **flow cytometry (FACS)**.

This allows each cell to be processed independently for single-cell library preparation.

---

## 4. Selective Removal of Newly Synthesized DNA

The BrdU-containing newly synthesized strands are selectively removed or rendered non-amplifiable during library preparation.

As a result, sequencing libraries are primarily generated from the **original parental template strands**.

This is the key principle that allows Strand-seq to preserve DNA strand directionality.

---

## 5. Library Preparation and Sequencing

The remaining parental template DNA is converted into sequencing libraries.

The libraries are then sequenced using short-read sequencing platforms.

Because only template strands are retained, sequencing reads preserve information about their **Watson or Crick orientation**.

---

## 6. Read Alignment and Strand Assignment

Sequencing reads are aligned to a reference genome.

Based on their mapping orientation, reads can be classified as originating from:

- **Watson (W) strand**
- **Crick (C) strand**

The strand-specific read distribution across chromosomes can then be analyzed.

---

## Strand States

For each chromosome pair in a single cell, three major strand-state patterns can be observed:

| Strand state | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **WW** | Both homologs contribute Watson template strands |
| **CC** | Both homologs contribute Crick template strands |
| **WC / CW** | One homolog contributes Watson and the other Crick |

These strand patterns provide genomic information that is not retained in conventional whole-genome sequencing.

---

## Applications

The strand-specific information generated by Strand-seq can be used for:

- **Structural variant (SV) detection**
- **Inversion detection**
- **Haplotype phasing**
- **Sister chromatid exchange (SCE) detection**
- **Chromosome mis-segregation analysis**
- **Genome assembly and correction**
- **Analysis of chromosome instability**

In particular, inversions can produce characteristic changes in Watson/Crick orientation, making Strand-seq especially useful for detecting and resolving structural variants.

---

## Core Principle

The overall Strand-seq workflow can be summarized as:

**DNA replication + BrdU incorporation  
→ Mitosis  
→ Single-cell isolation  
→ Selective removal of BrdU-containing newly synthesized strands  
→ Library preparation  
→ Sequencing  
→ Alignment  
→ Watson/Crick strand assignment**

> The defining feature of Strand-seq is the selective sequencing of parental template DNA strands, allowing strand directionality to be preserved at the single-cell level.
